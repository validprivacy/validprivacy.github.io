Metadata Removal

Photos, videos, and audio files carry hidden details: where they were made, on what device, and when. Remove them before you share. Metadata Removal runs locally, on your own device, and your files are never uploaded.

Drop files or folders here

JPEG, HEIC, PNG, WebP, MP4, MOV, M4A, MP3, WAV, and FLAC. Your originals are never changed.

How to use it

  1. Choose your files

    Pick files, pick a whole folder, or drag either onto the box. Folders inside folders are included.

  2. See what was hidden

    Each file is checked locally on your device, and the list shows what it was carrying, including GPS coordinates when a photo has them.

  3. Save clean copies

    Download them as one zip, or on Chrome and Edge, save them straight into a folder you choose. Pick an empty folder, since files with the same name there are replaced.

  4. Share the copies

    Share the clean copies and keep your originals. Your originals are never touched.

Why it matters

Metadata travels with a file wherever it goes, and most people never see it.

Where you were

Phones save GPS coordinates in photos and videos by default. A picture taken at home can lead a stranger to your address, and a few photos can map your home, work, school, and daily routine.

What you used

Camera make, model, lens, and sometimes a serial number. A serial number can tie photos posted on different accounts back to the same device and the same person.

When it happened

Exact dates and times, down to the second, show when you were somewhere and when you were away from home.

Who made it

Author and owner names, copyright fields, and the names typed into editing apps can carry your real name into a file you meant to share anonymously.

What you cut out

Some editors leave a small preview of the original inside the edited file, so a cropped photo can still hold a copy of the uncropped one. Editing history can also keep earlier file names and settings.

Audio and video too

Videos carry location, recording time, and device details. Audio files carry names, comments, encoder details, and embedded cover art.

Some apps remove part of this when you post, but many paths keep it: email attachments, cloud storage links, forums, marketplaces, and "send as file" in messaging apps all pass along the original file. Even when a platform strips metadata before others see it, the platform itself still received the original.

It cannot remove what is in the picture or recording itself: faces, street signs, reflections, landmarks, voices, or background sounds. Encoder settings written inside a video stream stay, because removing them would mean re-encoding the video. When you save a file, your computer gives the copy a new date of its own. Some drones save GPS in a separate .SRT file next to the video, so leave that file out when you share.

Coming soon: AVIF images, GIF, TIFF, RAW camera files, PDFs, and Word, Excel, and PowerPoint documents. Until then, those files are left out and listed as not supported, so nothing is ever passed through unclean.

Why this one

Fully local

Most "remove metadata online" sites upload your file to their server to clean it, which hands them the exact data you are trying to protect. This page does all of the work locally, inside your browser, and your files never leave your device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Lossless

Many tools clean a photo by saving it again, which lowers quality and can rotate phone photos sideways. This one cuts out only the metadata, so the picture, video, and audio stay byte for byte the same.

Action camera GPS removed

GoPro, DJI, and other action cameras and drones record GPS as its own track inside the video. Many tools only edit tags, so that track survives, and the usual workaround is rebuilding the video with a separate program. This one removes the track and blanks its data in place, leaving the video and audio untouched.

Whole folders, nothing to install

Desktop tools like ExifTool also work locally and are excellent, but they need installing and often the command line. This works in any modern browser, on whole folders at once, and shows you what it found before you save.

Run it with no internet at all

Download the offline version, a single file you open in any browser. It runs entirely on your computer with no connection, which suits organizations that keep sensitive files off the network.

Proof you can check
